# Impacts of Computing

**Suggested time:** 2-3 class periods  
**Connects to:** student data projects, file processing, OOP capstones, and
university enrichment

## Learning Goals

- Explain how a computing innovation can affect different stakeholders in
  different ways.
- Support a claim with credible evidence and acknowledge uncertainty.
- Identify a bias, accessibility, privacy, environmental, or equity concern.
- Propose a realistic design or policy improvement.

## Activity

1. Select a computing innovation with a clear local or personal connection.
   Examples include a navigation app, recommendation system, translation tool,
   online learning platform, medical wearable, public-service website, or
   automated decision system.
2. Identify at least three stakeholder groups. Describe one benefit and one
   possible harm or limitation for each group.
3. Research two credible sources. Record the publication, author or
   organization, date, and the claim supported by each source.
4. Create a claim-evidence-reasoning response:
   - **Claim:** State whether the innovation's benefits outweigh its harms in
     the context you chose.
   - **Evidence:** Cite the two sources and one observation from your
     stakeholder analysis.
   - **Reasoning:** Explain why the evidence supports the claim and name one
     uncertainty or limitation.
5. Propose one improvement. It may be a program feature, a policy, a
   disclosure, an accessibility change, or a data-practice change.

## Student Deliverable

Submit a one-page claim-evidence-reasoning response or a four-slide
presentation with a bibliography.

## Evidence Checklist

- Stakeholders include more than just the system's direct users.
- Benefits and harms are specific and supported by evidence.
- Sources are identified clearly enough for another person to locate them.
- The proposed improvement responds directly to a stated concern.
- The work distinguishes evidence from opinion.
